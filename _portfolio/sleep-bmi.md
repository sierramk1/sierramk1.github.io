This project examined whether short sleep duration is associated with higher body mass index (BMI) among U.S. adults aged 20 and older.

Using nationally representative, self-reported survey data, I conducted exploratory data analysis and multivariable regression modeling to evaluate the relationship between sleep duration and BMI while adjusting for demographic, behavioral, and mental health covariates.

We identified a nonlinear relationship between sleep duration and BMI and implemented a piecewise modeling strategy to examine the effects of low sleep (<7 hours per night). Log-transforming BMI improved residual normality and model stability.
