I am a second-year M.S. student in Biostatistics at the University of Michigan School of Public Health. I first became interested in the intersection of data science and public health through the course Data Science and Health Equity, where I saw how statistical and computational methods could be applied to questions in public health. This led me to pursue biostatistics, where I am interested in using statistical methods to better understand complex health data and contributing to collaborative public health research.

My research interests include high-dimensional data analysis, machine learning, data integration, survival analysis, and Bayesian methods. I am particularly interested in approaches that balance model complexity, stability, and interpretability. I am currently working on network-based statistical models for high-dimensional lipidomics data to study lipid signatures associated with chronic kidney disease.

### Skills

I have experience working with a range of programming languages and statistical computing tools. I primarily use R and Python for data analysis, statistical modeling, and visualization. My experience with R includes exploratory data analysis, regression modeling, data visualization, and working with high-dimensional biomedical data. In Python, I have worked with tools such as Pandas for data manipulation and analysis.

I also have experience with SQL (MySQL) for working with databases and extracting data, as well as SAS for statistical analysis. Through my coursework and research, I have developed experience with statistical methods including linear regression, generalized linear models, survival analysis, and machine learning.

For data visualization and reporting, I have worked with Apache Superset, Tableau, and Microsoft Excel, including developing interactive dashboards and presenting analytical results. I also use Git and GitHub for version control and collaborative work.

My programming background also includes Java, C++, and C, which has given me a strong foundation in programming concepts and computational problem-solving. I have also worked with HTML and CSS through web development projects.

### Education

Before graduate school, I earned a B.A. in Applied Mathematics with a concentration in Computer Science from Columbia University in the City of New York. My undergraduate coursework provided a foundation in mathematics, statistics, computer science, databases, and data analysis, which I now apply to problems in biostatistics and public health.

Outside of academics, I enjoy running, reading, listening to podcasts, trying new restaurants, and spending time with friends and family.
